Commitment to open research practice

Open science is often described as a set of tools—preprints, shared code, open data. I find it more useful to think of it as a set of habits that make work easier to check, build on, and correct.

Even before formal doctoral training, I am trying to adopt those habits: writing analyses so that another person can re-run them, preferring transparent assumptions over opaque defaults, and treating documentation as part of the research product rather than an afterthought. Where pre-registration or registered reports make sense for a question, I want to learn how to use them well.

None of this replaces judgment about privacy, sensitive data, or the timing of releases. It does mean defaulting toward clarity and shareability when those constraints allow. That orientation is one of the criteria I use when looking at research groups and programs.
